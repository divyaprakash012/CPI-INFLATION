> *A data analytics project exploring Consumer Price Index (CPI) trends across India’s rural, urban, and combined sectors — with a focus on food inflation, COVID-19, and fuel-related patterns.*

##  Objectives

- Examine CPI patterns across broad consumption categories.
- Compare the General Index across Rural, Urban, and Rural+Urban areas.
- Explore month-wise movement in vegetables, fruits, pulses, and milk.
- Compare food-category index values between 2022 and 2023, including rural and urban differences.
- Compare selected category trends before and after the COVID-19 onset period.
- Examine the relationship between Fuel & Light and transport, education, food and beverages, household goods and services, non-alcoholic beverages, and cereals.
- Present findings in a clear, business-friendly format.

##  Tools & Skills Used

- *Microsoft Excel* — data analysis and reporting
- *Power Query* — data transformation and unpivoting
- *PivotTables and PivotCharts* — category-wise summaries and comparisons
- *Data cleaning and reshaping* — preparing data for analysis
- *Averages and percentage change* — comparing index movements
- *Correlation analysis* — checking how selected series move together
- *Data visualization and insight writing* — communicating findings
-*Interactive Slicers*: Excel slicers enable users to dynamically filter CPI data by year, category, and other dimensions for quick and interactive analysis.

##  Overall Project Insights

1. *Broad upward trend:* The Rural+Urban General Index average rises from about 129.10 in 2016 to 177.57 in 2023.
2. *COVID-period comparison:* Food & Beverages, Health, and Household Goods & Services all show higher average index values in 2020 than in 2019.
3. *Food-category differences:* Spices and cereals show notable increases in the 2023 averages, while vegetables and oils and fats show declines compared with 2022.
4. *Volatile vegetables:* The monthly vegetable index peaks in October 2022 in the displayed series before dropping in November and December.
5. *Fuel correlations vary:* Fuel is positively correlated with Non-alcoholic Beverages and Transport in the workbook summary, but weakly negatively correlated with Food & Beverages and Cereals & Products.
6. *Correlation is not causation:* These comparisons reveal patterns in the selected data but do not prove that fuel prices or COVID-19 caused the observed changes.

##  Conclusion

This Excel-based project demonstrates how CPI data can be transformed into useful comparisons across years, locations, and consumption categories. The analysis highlights an upward trend in index levels, differences among food categories, increases in selected indices around the COVID-19 period, and varying relationships between fuel and other categories.

The project strengthened practical skills in Excel-based data preparation, PivotTable analysis, trend comparison, correlation, and communicating data insights.
